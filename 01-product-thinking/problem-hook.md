## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** Cold read of my hook, as a skeptical stakeholder:

What lands: The rising SSR next to rising broken plans is the strongest signal. The north star looks better while outcomes get worse.
What doesn’t land:
“Only 3.8% of outreach ends in an agreement” is a weak number. Many consumers in the outreach do not need or want an instalment plan, so a skeptic would say the denominator is wrong. The better base is the 8,360 starters, of whom 46% reach an agreement.
There is no money in the hook: no euros of lost recovery, no agent cost, no client revenue at risk.
The link between the 6-month-first screen and the missed payments is a correlation in six months of fictional data, not proof.

Risk assessment:

Not minor: My own calculation from the data pack is about 2,600 agent contacts per month, roughly 500 handling hours. More than half are about questions the journey could answer. Missed payments rose from 15.2% to 19.0%, which is about 146 extra broken plans per month on 3,846 agreements. Clients see broken plans in their reporting (N-10).
Not proven high-stakes: The pack has no financial figures, no client losses, no complaint data and no regulatory finding. Nothing says a client is leaving or a regulator is involved.
Verdict: Strong enough for a focused pilot, not strong enough to call it a crisis. The hook should not use crisis language.
- **Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:** Systemic. The pain shows up in every segment of the data pack, and the biggest pain points affect large groups, not rare cases.
Evidence for systemic (fictional data):

Same level of pain everywhere. Agent contact within 7 days is 30.4% to 31.5% across entry channels, devices and portfolio types. If the problem were an edge case, these groups would differ much more.
Large groups, not small ones. Consumers with two or more open claims are 2,968 of 8,360 starters (35%). Variable or shift income is 2,862 (34%). The mobile upload bug touched 853 journeys (about 10%).
Many causes, one pattern. The top three contact reasons (missed payment, combining claims, why income is asked) are 52% of contacts, and all three are unanswered questions in the journey. This points to a design gap, not one bug.
Broken plans are common in all segments. Missed payments within 60 days are 17.4% to 22.5% in every segment, with the highest rates for variable income (22.5%) and multi-claim consumers (about 21%).
Interviews agree. Consumers (N-01, N-03, N-06), agents (N-08, N-09) and compliance (N-12) all describe the same missing explanations and visibility.
- **Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?:** As I wrote it, the value proposition was too generic. It restated the brief’s challenge. It only removes the obstacle once it is tied to specific fixes for the specific pain points.
Cold read of my value proposition: “Help consumers agree to an affordable, sustainable plan with a clear handoff” could describe any instalment tool. A skeptical stakeholder would ask: what exactly changes for Mara?
