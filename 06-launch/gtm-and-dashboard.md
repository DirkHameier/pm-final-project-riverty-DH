# GTM Launch Plan, Riverty BU Collection, A Fair Way Forward

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Feature | B2: Plain explainer for why income is asked and how it is used. At the income question, a short "Why do we ask?" panel (max. 60 words) is open by default. It explains which income to enter ("If your pay changes, enter a normal month") and how the data is used and how long it is kept. |
| Goal | Conversion |
| Launch tier | M, Targeted |

## Goal & Audience
- **Goal:** Conversion, The feature is already live in the journey, and the problem is drop-off, not awareness. Only 34.8% of multi-claim starters reach an agreement (52.1% with one claim). Mara reaches the income question and does not know why it is asked or which income to enter. The explainer should move one business metric: the agreement rate. It also supports the north star, the Self-Service Rate (34.7% today).
- **Target audience:** Primary: consumers who start an instalment journey with two or more open claims, like Mara (38, shift worker, on her phone). About 2,968 starters per month, and most use mobile.
Secondary: starters with variable or shift income (about 2,862 per month). Their agreement rate is lower (41.6%) and their missed payments are higher (22.5%).

## Launch Tier
- **M, Targeted**, Reach: the feature affects one step in the journey, for about 2,968 multi-claim starters per month (8,360 starters in total). It is not a new product, and consumers already come through reminders and the portal. 

Revenue impact: a +4 point gain in multi-claim agreement rate means roughly 120 more agreements per month. This is useful but not large enough for press or events. 

Risk of silence: low. Consumers see the explainer directly at the income question, so they do not need to know about it in advance. The real risk is for agents and compliance: they must know about the new text, the data-use line and the "enter a normal month" rule. So I choose focused comms to the pilot segment and the agent team, not a multi-channel campaign. L or XL would over-invest, and S would leave agents and compliance unprepared.

## Channels
1. **Owned: In-portal explainer at the income question. This is where Mara is already deciding, on her phone, so every journey starter sees it with no extra outreach.**
2. **Owned: Existing reminder messages (email, SMS, letter with QR code). One added line, e.g. "Set up a plan in a few minutes. If your pay changes, enter a normal month." These three channels bring in all 8,360 monthly starters (email 3,770, SMS 2,543, letter 2,047).**
3. **Owned: Agent conversations. Agents point callers to the digital plan and the explainer, using a short talking point. This reaches consumers who call before they start the journey.**

## Enablement & Assets
Agents: one-page briefing and a 2-3 line talking point before launch, plus an FAQ ("why do you ask for income?", "which income do I enter?"). Compliance: approval of the German text and the data-use line (purpose and retention period). Support/CS: updated help-centre text with the same wording. Assets to build: explainer panel copy (max. 60 words), one added line for email/SMS/letter, tracking events (explainer opened, income step completed). Brief agents and compliance before launch, so nobody is surprised by the new text.

## Ownership, Budget & Timeline
- **Ownership & budget:** Product Manager (me) owns the plan, the experiment and the post-launch metrics. UX Writer owns the explainer text (max. 60 words) and the extra line in email, SMS and letter. Compliance Officer owns approval of the German text and the data-use line (purpose and retention period). Engineering Lead owns the build, the A/B split and the tracking events. Collections Team Lead owns the agent briefing and talking point. Budget: no paid media. Extra cost is internal effort only (writing, compliance review, small build). Asset gap: the retention period must be confirmed by compliance before launch.
- **Timeline:** Phase 1, pilot prep (weeks 1-2): text written, compliance approval, build and tracking ready, agents briefed. Phase 2, launch (weeks 3-9): A/B test starts, 50/50 split, with the explainer shown to pilot starters; reminder messages get the extra line; weekly check of the guardrails. Phase 3, post-launch (weeks 10-16): 60-day follow-up for missed payments ends, results are reviewed, and go/no-go is decided. If the result is positive, roll out to all starters and consider B1 (one plan for several claims) as the next step.

## Success Metrics
- **Metrics:** 1. Agreement rate of multi-claim starters (primary): 34.8% → 38.8% or higher (+4 points).
2. Self-Service Rate, plans only (supporting): 34.7% baseline, expected to rise with more agreements and no extra agent contact.
3. Income step completion rate (leading signal): share of starters who finish the income question after seeing the explainer.
- **Bad signal to watch for:** Agreements rise but missed payments go above 19.0% (22.5% for variable income), or agent contact goes above 31.0%. Then the explainer only pushes people into plans they cannot keep.
- **Likely post-launch decision:** Iterate: if agreement rate rises by at least +4 points and guardrails hold, roll out to all starters. If the rise is smaller, test new wording. Stop if guardrails break.
