Slide 5 · Strategy

Problem, value and the hypothesis behind B2

Problem statement hook

Consumers like Mara abandon or fail Riverty’s digital instalment journey, so only 3.8% of outreach ends in an agreement and 19% of those plans break within 60 days. This raises agent workload and client complaints, and the SSR north star can still look better than reality.

Chosen path: BU Collection
Value proposition

Shift workers like Mara often leave the instalment journey at the income question, because they do not know why we ask or which amount to enter. A short “Why do we ask?” panel explains the purpose, says “If your pay changes, enter a normal month”, and shows how the data is used.

Mara can finish a fair plan on her phone without calling an agent. Riverty gets more agreements and fewer calls, and keeps missed payments at or below 19.0%.

Data-backed hypothesis

I believe that a clear “Why do we ask?” explainer at the income question will help consumers with two or more open claims, like Mara, finish the journey. The agreement rate should rise by 4 percentage points (34.8% to 38.8%) within 16 weeks. Missed payments within 60 days must stay at or below 19.0%, and agent contact within 7 days at or below 31.0%.

N-01: “I did not know if they meant this month or a normal month” 34.8% agreement, 2+ claims (52.1% with one claim) 1,421 lost (19.3%) after affordability questions Missed payments 22.5% variable vs 17.4% fixed

Slide 6 · Research, competitive analysis & journey map

Mara’s workaround, and where it breaks

Role & goal

Consumer – a shift worker with two open claims who opens the portal on her phone during a short break. She wants one affordable plan for both claims without having to call anyone.

External tools

The research pack does not name any external tool for Mara. Her workaround is manual.

Evidence

N-01 “I opened it on my break. It asked for my income and I did not know if they meant this month or a normal month. I closed it and meant to come back.”

T-206 No help text explains why income is asked or how long it is kept. Variable-income consumers reach an agreement less often (41.6% vs 48.1%) and miss payments more often (22.5% vs 17.4%).

Workaround, step by step
  • She opens the portal on her phone during a break and has only a few minutes (N-01).
  • She reaches the income question and cannot answer it. No help text explains why it is asked (T-206).
  • A · mental bookmark: she closes the portal and means to come back. The back button can even clear her answers (T-204).
  • B · phone call: N-04 “A person can tell me if this is going to cost me more.” On the call she repeats everything, because the agent cannot see what she typed (N-08, T-205).
  • C · the guess (partly an assumption): she guesses “this month or normal month”. Supported by N-02, where a plan looked right and the second payment was missed.
Core frustration

Mara reaches the income question and cannot answer it. Her pay changes with her shifts, and nothing on the screen explains what to enter or why the question is asked, so she stops.

Journey map · mara-future-state-journey.md
Open

Mara gets a reminder (email, SMS or letter with QR code). On a short break she opens the portal on her phone and starts the instalment journey. (8,360 starters per month, 6,563 on mobile)

Scan

She sees her two open claims. She wants one plan she can afford, without calling anyone. (2,968 starters per month have 2 or more claims)

Hesitate

She reaches the income question. Her pay changes with her shifts, and nothing explains why we ask or whether to enter this month or a normal month (T-206, N-01).

Bounce · moment of misery

She is afraid of typing a wrong number and closes the portal. Of 7,364 people at the affordability questions, only 5,943 reach the next step: 1,421 lost (19.3%). “Why income is asked” is 16.2% of all contacts.

Where we intervene

At the income question, a “Why do we ask?” panel (max. 60 words) is open by default. Mara answers with confidence and finishes the plan (B2).

The “no” I protect: I will not raise the Self-Service Rate with changes that push consumers into plans they cannot keep.

PRD · vision

To eliminate Mara’s guesswork and fear at the income question by explaining in plain words why we ask, which income to enter and how her answer is used.

Prototype: 04-roadmap/prototype.html
Screens & key requirements
  • Entry point · Income question · Confirmation
  • Explainer open by default, within the first screen on 360 × 640 px
  • Max. 60 words, sentences of at most 15 words
  • Label “Typical monthly net income (€)”; helper “If your pay changes, enter a normal month.”
  • Data-use line with purpose and retention period [to be confirmed by compliance]
Scope & guardrails

Must: plain help text · clear income rule · data-use statement · compliance approval of the German text.

Should: missed-payment line · “Talk to an agent” link · tracking events.

Won’t (now): B3 affordability · scoring · B1 multi-claim · B5 · B8 · other markets.

Safety: fixed, reviewed text. Never promises outcomes. An unconfirmed retention period blocks release.

Slide 8 · Validation

A/B experiment: one change at the income question

Hypothesis

Plain “Why do we ask?” explainer for Mara (38, shift worker, variable income, two open claims) → more multi-claim consumers answer the income question with confidence and finish the journey with a plan they can keep, instead of closing the portal.

Primary metricAgreement rate, starters with 2+ open claims
Baseline34.8%
MDE+4 pp (34.8% → 38.8%)
Sample / arm2,281 · 50/50 split
Duration16 weeks · p < 0.05
GuardrailsMissed payment within 60 days ≤ 19.0% overall, ≤ 22.5% variable income · agent contact within 7 days ≤ 31.0%
BoundaryInvestigate if variant is more than 1 point above control; stop if more than 3 points above
Control (A)

Today’s income screen with no help text. Nothing explains why income is asked, what to enter if pay changes, or how data is used (T-206). 7,364 reach the affordability questions, 5,943 the next step: 1,421 lost (19.3%).

Variant (B)

One change: “Why do we ask?” panel open by default (max. 60 words), label “Typical monthly net income (€)”, helper line, and a data-use line. Plans, amounts and plan order stay the same. Randomised by consumer ID.

Ship≥ +4 pp at p < 0.05, guardrails hold
IteratePositive, but below the MDE
KillNo improvement, or negative

The read date is fixed at the end of 16 weeks. No results are reviewed before this date.

Slide 10 · Story, individual insights

What was hard, what clicked, what comes next

Hard

The data did not give one simple answer. The 6-month-first plan screen raised the Self-Service Rate (31.1% → 34.7%), but missed payments rose too (15.2% → 19.0%). A metric can go up while the product gets worse. Chat users also looked better (SSR 41.5% vs. 33.2%), but they may be a self-selected group, so I could not treat it as cause and effect.

Surprised me

The best first feature was not a big build. The biggest friction was a missing text at the income question, and it needs only a small change. This one screen explains a 19.3% drop (1,421 people) and 16.2% of agent contacts.

Aha moment

I stopped asking “what can we build?” and started asking “what can we measure and isolate?”. Once I wrote the A/B test with one change, the same plans, the same order and randomisation by consumer ID, the feature, the metric and the guardrails became one clear chain.

Key takeaways
  • Guardrails are part of the metric, not an extra. Without the 19.0% missed-payment limit, the 6-month screen would have looked like a success.
  • Good evidence needs clean instrumentation: events like “explainer opened” and “income step completed” must exist before launch, or we cannot learn anything.
  • Small, isolated changes are easier to test, ship and roll back than big ones.
What I would do next
  • Run the B2 A/B test (50/50, 16 weeks) and read the result only at the end.
  • Fix the mobile upload bug (B5, ticket T-201), because 853 journeys never got a document.
  • Prepare B1 (one plan for several claims) with an early technical check of claim merging across backends, and B3 (affordability check) with simple rules that compliance can explain.
