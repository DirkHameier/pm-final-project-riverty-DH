# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Consumer - A shift worker with two open claims who opens the portal on her phone during a short break.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Set up one affordable plan for both claims without having to call anyone
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** The portal asks for her income, and she does not know if it means this month or a normal month (N-01). She closes it, and nothing in the journey explains why the question is asked (T-206). Her situation is common, and the numbers are worse for people like her:
Variable-income consumers miss payments more often (22.5% vs 17.4% for fixed salary).
Consumers with two claims reach an agreement less often (37.7% vs 52.1% for one claim).
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** The research pack does not name any external tool Mara. But switching to the phone
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Mara’s workaround, step by step
She opens the portal on her phone during a break (N-01). She has only a few minutes.
She reaches the income question and cannot answer it. She does not know if “income” means this month or a normal month (N-01). No help text explains why it is asked or how long the data is kept (T-206).
Workaround A, the “mental bookmark”: she closes the portal and means to come back (N-01). Nothing in the journey saves her place or reminds her, and the back button on the plan screen can even clear her answers (T-204).
Workaround B, the phone call: the case brief says that when the journey confuses her or asks for documents she cannot find, she calls an agent. N-04 confirms the preference: “A person can tell me if this is going to cost me more.”
On the call, she repeats everything. The agent cannot see what she typed online (N-08, T-205), so identity, income and case details are asked again.
Workaround C, the guess (risk, partly an assumption): if she continues without a call, she must guess “this month or normal month”. This is not directly quoted for Mara. It is supported by N-02, where another consumer chose a plan that looked right and then missed the second payment.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The moment Mara reaches the income question and cannot answer it. The portal asks for her income, but she does not know if it means this month or a normal month. Her pay changes with her shifts, and she has only a short break. Nothing on the screen explains what to enter or why the question is asked, so she stops.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** The evidence
Quote (N-01, consumer, two open claims, shift work): “I opened it on my break. It asked for my income and I did not know if they meant this month or a normal month. I closed it and meant to come back.”
Supporting bug or ticket (T-206): No help text explains why income is asked or how long it is kept.
Supporting behavior in the data: Consumers with variable or shift income reach an agreement less often (41.6% vs 48.1% for fixed salary) and miss payments more often (22.5% vs 17.4%).
