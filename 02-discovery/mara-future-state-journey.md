# Future State Journey: Mara, Digital Instalment Plan

*Sample data, illustrative, not Riverty research. All people, numbers and events are fictional.*

## Persona

**Consumer (Mara):** A shift worker with two open claims who opens the portal on her phone during a short break.

- **Goal:** Set up one affordable plan for both claims without having to call anyone.
- **Friction:** The portal asks for her income, and she does not know if it means this month or a normal month (N-01). She closes it, and nothing in the journey explains why the question is asked (T-206).
- **Why it matters:** Variable-income consumers miss payments more often (22.5% vs 17.4% for fixed salary). Consumers with two claims reach an agreement less often (37.7% vs 52.1% for one claim).

## Strategy

The case is moving instalment agreements from agent-led phone handling to a digital self-service journey in the portal, one that helps consumers agree to an affordable plan they can keep, with a clear, explainable handoff to an agent when they need one.

## Value proposition

A digital instalment journey that helps consumers like Mara agree to a plan they can afford and keep, with a clear handoff to an agent when needed.

- **Fewer drop-offs and plans that last:** Today only 3.8% of outreach ends in an agreement, and 19% of new plans miss a payment within 60 days.
- **Less avoidable agent work:** About 31% of starters call an agent, often for questions the journey could answer.
- **Fair, explainable outcomes:** Plans fit variable income, low-confidence cases go to a human, and decisions can be explained.
- **Honest measurement:** SSR is paired with plan-kept and complaint measures.

| **Mara feels** | Rushed, wary | Unsure, then reassured | Careful, then in control | Confident, no surprises |

**Available at every stage:** Talk to an agent who sees Mara's entries and why the plan was offered (T-205, N-08).

## The 4 stages

Format: Action -> Benefit (max 15 words per bullet). "Internal State" means Mara's thoughts and feelings.

### 1. Arrive and see both claims

- **User Action:** Opens the reminder link on her phone -> sees both claims together.
- **Internal State:** Rushed and wary -> a short, calm intro builds trust.
- **Pain Point Addressed:** Claims shown one at a time (T-202, N-03) -> one combined view.

### 2. Share income with guidance

- **User Action:** Chooses "this month" or "usual month" -> answers without guessing.
- **Internal State:** Afraid of typing a wrong number (N-06) -> sees why it is asked.
- **Pain Point Addressed:** Unclear income question (N-01, T-206) -> plain wording, help text, saved answers (T-204).

### 3. Choose one fitting plan

- **User Action:** Compares plan options for both claims -> picks one that fits her pay.
- **Internal State:** Unsure which plan is safe (N-02) -> monthly cost shown in plain words.
- **Pain Point Addressed:** Shortest plan looks "right" but breaks -> affordable fit is shown first.

### 4. Confirm and stay on track

- **User Action:** Reads what happens after a missed payment -> confirms without surprises.
- **Internal State:** Wants certainty -> gets a summary and an on-time reminder (T-207).
- **Pain Point Addressed:** Chat approves plans without consequences (T-208) -> risks explained before she agrees.

## 3 competitive advantages over the manual workaround

The manual workaround is: leave the portal, then call an agent.

1. **Fits her break:** Finishes or resumes on her phone -> no waiting for a call.
2. **No repeated questions:** Agent sees her entries and the plan reason -> faster, calmer calls (N-08, T-205).
3. **Plans she can keep:** One explained plan for both claims -> fewer broken plans (19% today).

## Notes

- The stages are a proposal based on the research and are not yet tested with users.
- Source references: N = interview notes, T = tickets and bugs in the BU Collection data pack.
